## 1. How comparable publications sustain themselves

| Publication | Model | What the evidence says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Pudding / Polygraph | Visual essays as marketing for a client studio; small Patreon | 2017 Nieman Lab: essays "essentially serve as marketing" for client work. No public revenue figures. |
| Our World in Data | Grants plus reader donations (UK charity) | 4,000+ individual donors by 2023; grants from SFF ($300k, 2024) and Coefficient Giving ($3m, 2025). |
| Aeon / Psyche | Reader donations, nonprofit, ad-free, no paywall | Patron tier above $500; no public figures found. |
| Kurzgesagt | Merchandise ~40–45%, YouTube ads ~13%, Patreon ~7–9%, sponsors ~11–12%, grants ~10–13% | Studio's own 2023 disclosure; ~65% of income directly from viewers. |
| The Browser | Paid curation newsletter | 10,000+ paying subscribers at $34–49/yr by 2019; team of two or three. |
| Tangle | Nonpartisan politics newsletter; 85% subscriptions, 15% ads | $4.15m revenue 2025; 500k+ subscribers; converts about 16% of free to paid (founder figures via Press Gazette). Growth reported slowing. |
| Defector | Reader subscriptions (85%+), later ads, consulting, Apple News+ | $3.8m year two; subscriber count flat; retention and win-back named as the problem. |
| Ground News | Freemium subscription, no institutional investors | Bias labels licensed from third parties; criticised for oversimplification. |

**Benchmark:** median free-to-paid conversion on Substack is about 3%; large general lists 1–2%; above 5% is strong. Tangle's 16% is an outlier built on a daily habit and a clear civic promise.

**Inference (ours):** no one in this set makes money from traffic alone. Every sustainable example has either (a) a direct relationship with readers who pay or donate, (b) a studio or client business that the public work advertises, or (c) grants. A new entrant needs a plausible path to one of these within eighteen months.

## 2. Why people engage with intellectually demanding content

- **Arousal, not valence.** Berger & Milkman (2012), 7,000 NYT articles: content that evokes high-arousal emotion (awe, anger, anxiety) is shared more; sadness less. Practical value and interest also lift sharing. Implication: a Palimpsest piece needs an awe-or-indignation moment with a number in it, not a mood of calm reflection.
- **The self as subject.** Instruments that return a personal result (a timing, a receipt, a verdict) convert reading into something the reader can report. This is why personalised results are shared and essays are not. (Hypothesis, supported by the virality literature and the success of personalised result formats; not measured for Palimpsest yet.)
- **Discovery before reading.** Reuters Institute DNR 2025: engagement with traditional sources keeps falling; social video and aggregators grow; only ~18% in richer countries pay for news. Implication: long-form must be *found* via short-form and shares, and must earn an email address because platforms do not deliver repeat readers.

## 4. Where the concept is weak (candid)

1. **Crowded adjacent space.** Explainers about attention and belief exist at Vox, Aeon, Kurzgesagt and in bestselling books. Palimpsest's differentiation has to be the *form* (an instrument you use on yourself, with registers of certainty) and the *stance* (challenging both sides), not the topic.
2. **Production cost.** Each investigation is a week-plus of research, writing and engineering. Cadence will be slow. Slow cadence is survivable only with a strong email relationship and pieces that keep being shared for months.
3. **No distribution asset.** Zero audience, zero budget. The pieces must carry their own distribution via personalised shareable results and short-form adaptation.
4. **Unproven willingness to pay.** Nothing in the research says people pay for *interactive essays*. They pay for daily habits (Tangle), identity (Defector), or curation (The Browser). Monetisation, if any, will likely be donations plus a studio/commission business, as with The Pudding and OWID.
